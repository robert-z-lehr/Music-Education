HOW WE LEARN

Quality practice, not perfect beginnings.

Small chunks

Practice the smallest useful phrase instead of repeatedly restarting the entire piece.

Slow enough to notice

Choose a tempo where you can hear, feel, and correct what your hands are doing.

Repeat success

After fixing a mistake, repeat the corrected version so the correction becomes the habit.

Listen while playing

Your ears are part of the feedback loop. Do not let your fingers practice unsupervised.

LESSON PATH

From first melody to musical independence

Use these in order at first. Later, branch toward classical, pop, composition, improvisation, jazz, film/game music, or ear playing.

PRACTICE LAB

Isolate one variable. Then rebuild.

Pitch, rhythm, tempo, dynamics, articulation, phrasing, fingering, and coordination can be practiced separately before combining them.

Five-pass practice

  1. ListenCan you sing or tap the phrase before touching the keys?
  2. SimplifyChoose 2–5 notes, one hand, or rhythm alone.
  3. SlowPlay at a tempo where quality is controllable.
  4. RepeatGet three calm, correct repetitions.
  5. ReconnectAdd the neighboring phrase or missing musical variable.

Focus generator

THE GOAL

Read it. Hear it. Play it. Change it.

A student is becoming independent when they can notice a problem, isolate it, correct it, verify the correction by ear, and reconnect it to the music.
